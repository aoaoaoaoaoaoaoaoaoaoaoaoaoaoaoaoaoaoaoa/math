Techniques

  • Fixed-Boundary Correspondence Zero-run synchronization proves GPCP(4); a repeated rank-one separator gives M₃(5).
  • Paired-Role Compression A shared word channel gives Z₄(3); a common-fixed-column converse gives M₄(4).
  • Binary Compilers Shared partial state gives Z₆(2); prefix decoding and common-image restriction give M₁₀(2).
  • The Valuation Guard Rank-two returns reduce a concrete M₃(2) family to one deterministic p-adic orbit.

What is matrix mortality?

For fixed positive integers d and k, Md(k) asks: given at most k integer d × d matrices, does some nonempty product equal the zero matrix?

Ai1 Ai2 ⋯ Ain = 0, n≥1.

The matrix entries and witness length are unbounded. Multiplication retains the order of the selected generators, so a product is a word over a finite noncommutative alphabet.

Mortality is recursively enumerable: enumerate products by length and stop when zero appears. Undecidability means that no algorithm terminates correctly on every mortal and nonmortal input.

Two related problems retain only one scalar coefficient. Zd(k) supplies a row L and column C and asks whether LYC = 0 for some product Y of at most k integer d × d matrices. Rd(k) fixes the coefficient to one matrix corner. The structured form Z̊ additionally requires every generator to have first column e1.

Proof dependencies

The fixed-boundary theorem supplies four structured word-pair matrices. The remaining results use that interface:

  • Rank-one boundary absorption gives M3(5).
  • An anti-diagonal quotient of the paired role channels gives Z4(3), followed by M4(4).
  • A shared-partial-channel binary compiler gives Z6(2), followed by R7(2).
  • A complete binary prefix decoder and ten-dimensional common-image restriction give M10(2).
  • A three-mode return family turns arbitrary positive waits into one deterministic p-adic recurrence without adding a separator generator.

Bounded frontier

Undecidability is monotone in both parameters. Extra generators may be ignored, and dimension padding preserves mortality:

A↦A⊕0.

After the three techniques above and the established reductions of Cassaigne, Halava, Harju, and Nicolas, the currently established Pareto-minimal undecidable points are

Z: Z3(4),Z4(3),Z6(2); R: R3(5),R4(4),R5(3),R7(2); M: M3(5),M4(4),M6(3),M10(2).
