## Synopsis

The paper constructs continued-fraction algorithms designed to converge simultaneously in one
real and one p-adic embedding. The partial quotient is selected using both places, and the
convergents satisfy quantitative real and p-adic approximation estimates whose exponents add to
two. This two-place selection differs essentially from algorithms defined by a p-adic floor
alone.

The proposed algorithm terminates for every rational input. For quadratic inputs the paper
proves periodicity results in specified cases, including an eventual-periodicity theorem at
`p=2`, and supplies numerical examples. The rational proof uses an Archimedean descent created by
the simultaneous choice rule; p-adic convergence by itself would not yield the same conclusion.

The results therefore demonstrate that a controlled real selector can convert a p-adic
expansion into a terminating Euclidean process, but do not cover arbitrary prescribed digit
sequences or matrix-valued cocycles.
