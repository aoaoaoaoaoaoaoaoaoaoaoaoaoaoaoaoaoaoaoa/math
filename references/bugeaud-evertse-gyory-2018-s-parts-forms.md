## Synopsis

The paper bounds the part supported on a fixed finite prime set `S` of values taken by
univariate polynomials, binary forms, and decomposable forms at primitive integral points. For a
polynomial with at least two distinct roots, it records an effective estimate
`[f(x)]ₛ ≤ κ₂|f(x)|^(1−κ₁)`. Under squarefreeness, a sharper but ineffective exponent arbitrarily
close to the reciprocal degree follows from the p-adic Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem.

For binary forms, effective power savings require at least three pairwise nonproportional linear
factors over the splitting field. The decomposable-form results impose corresponding rank and
connectivity conditions. The effective constants ultimately use complex and p-adic logarithmic
forms; sharper asymptotic exponents use the Subspace Theorem. Density estimates describe how
often unusually large `S`-parts occur.

Every theorem fixes both `S` and the polynomial or form. A moving family whose coefficients
grow at the same scale as the evaluation point is outside the stated hypotheses.
