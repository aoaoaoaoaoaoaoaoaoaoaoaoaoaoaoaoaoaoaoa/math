## Synopsis

The paper enlarges number-field P-adic continued fractions by allowing partial quotients to have
denominators at a fixed finite set `T` of places besides the distinguished place `P`. This
removes ideal-class obstructions that occur when all partial quotients must be integral away
from `P`, and connects finite continued fractions with division chains in number fields.

Theorem 4.1 extends the earlier adelic height criterion. Its `ντ` includes the distinguished
P-adic expansion, every Archimedean continuant factor, and the nonnegative contribution from
each allowed extraneous denominator place. Again `ντ≤1` bounds complete-quotient height and
forces finiteness or periodicity, while `ντ<1` forces termination; the proof also gives explicit
bounds for the length or period once the constants are effective.

The constructive results show that every number field admits a finite denominator set `T` for
which suitable algorithms are finite at all but finitely many prime ideals, with explicit
families and bounds in several cases. The denominator set must be fixed in advance. The theorem
does not apply to a process whose new prime support grows with the orbit.
