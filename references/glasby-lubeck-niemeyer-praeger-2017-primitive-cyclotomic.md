## Synopsis

The paper studies the largest strong primitive divisor `Φ⁎ₙ(q)` of `qⁿ−1`: the full product,
with multiplicity, of those prime powers coprime to every earlier difference `qᵏ−1`. For
`n>2`, Lemma 7 identifies it exactly as either `Φₙ(q)` or `Φₙ(q)/r`, where `r` is the largest
prime divisor of `n`; the exponent-two case requires removing the full two-part of `q+1`.

The paper proves elementary cyclotomic size estimates, including
`q^φ(n)/4 < Φₙ(q) < 4q^φ(n)`, and uses them with arithmetic restrictions on primitive primes to
enumerate all prime-power pairs for which the strong primitive part lies below a prescribed
polynomial threshold. The resulting tables extend and correct cases omitted from Hering's
earlier classification.

The definition retains prime-power multiplicity and is therefore stronger than a squarefree
primitive radical. Its exact formula is special to the cyclotomic sequence `qⁿ−1`; it does not
bound the radical of a general integer sequence.
