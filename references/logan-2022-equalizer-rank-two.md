## Synopsis

For a set `S` of homomorphisms from the rank-two free group `F(a,b)` to a finitely generated
free group, Theorem 1.2 classifies the possible rank behavior of the equalizer. If every map is
injective, the equalizer has rank at most two. If the set contains both injective and
noninjective maps, its equalizer has rank at most one. If no map is injective, the equalizer is
not finitely generated. Examples realize every permitted rank.

The proof passes through stable domains. When one map is injective, the equalizer is the fixed
subgroup of an endomorphism of the stable domain, bounding its rank by the rank of that domain.
Inertness hypotheses then control the stable-domain chain. For two injective maps, Theorem 1.4
shows that an inert image bounds equalizer rank by the source rank, while two inert images make
the equalizer itself inert. The rank-two theorem follows because every two-generated subgroup of
a free group is inert.

For injective homomorphisms whose images are retracts of their joint image, Theorem 1.5 gives a
strict rank drop when the images differ. Theorem 1.6 gives an algorithm computing a basis of the
equalizer in this retract-image stratum. Later corollaries extend the inert and retract results to
sets of maps and to induced pairs.

The paper proves structural rank bounds in rank two, not a general algorithm for testing
equalizer triviality. Its basis algorithm assumes the retract-image hypotheses. It also records
that the unrestricted free-group Post correspondence problem was open at the time; Carvalho's
2026 preprint supersedes that historical statement in unbounded source rank.

## Project Use

The rank-two theorem makes a binary-domain free-group equalizer a sharply structured object but
does not decide whether it is trivial. It therefore neither supplies nor rules out the positive
binary fixed-boundary compiler isolated in the `M₄(3)` free-cancellation route.
