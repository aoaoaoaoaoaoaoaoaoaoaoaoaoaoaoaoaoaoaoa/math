## Synopsis

Theorem 1.3 proves the dynamical Mordell–Lang conclusion for an étale endomorphism `Φ` of a
quasiprojective variety in characteristic zero: for any point `α` and closed subvariety `V`,
the hitting-time set `{n ≥ 0 | Φⁿ(α) ∈ V}` is a finite union of complete arithmetic
progressions together with a finite set. Equivalently, the orbit–subvariety intersection is a
finite union of suborbits.

The proof spreads the data to a finitely generated ring, chooses a suitable nonarchimedean
place, and interpolates subsequences of the orbit by convergent `p`-adic analytic functions.
Theorem 3.3 supplies the analytic interpolation under congruence conditions. Zero isolation for
the resulting analytic functions yields Theorem 4.1 for unramified maps, after which algebraic
geometry extends the result to étale maps and nonsmooth varieties.

The theorem concerns powers of one map. It does not provide an interpolation of arbitrary words
in a noncommutative semigroup or of independently chosen exponent blocks without an additional
commutation or finite-skeleton reduction.

## Source Assessment

The retained record distinguishes the 2008 arXiv preprint from the 2010 journal article. No
correction or retraction was found. The étale hypothesis and single-orbit quantifier are
essential scope conditions, not technical details that may be dropped in a matrix-semigroup
application.
