## Synopsis

Colbert studies finite and infinite union-closed families through inclusion-chain conditions.
Abundance is formulated by an injection from members omitting a coordinate into members
containing it. A coordinate is optimal when its incidence subfamily is inclusion-maximal among
coordinate-incidence subfamilies. Descending chain condition for the family yields ascending
chain condition for these incidence subfamilies and hence optimal coordinates.

The central finite-height theorem says that every nontrivial union-closed family of poset
dimension at most two has an abundant coordinate; here “dimension” means maximum chain
length, not order dimension. The proof reduces to a separating family, shows that every member
omitting an optimal coordinate has a distinct cover containing it, and obtains the abundance
injection. The paper extends Tian's finite height-three result to infinite families. It also proves
abundance for topological spaces whose open-set poset satisfies the descending chain condition
and gives a domination theorem for unions with tent families.

## Source Assessment

This is the peer-reviewed version of record. No source-level defect material to the project use
was found. The unconventional term “dimension” is defined explicitly as maximum finite chain
length; confusing it with order dimension or with chain cardinality produces an off-by-one rank
error.

## Project Use

The dimension-two theorem corroborates the reduced low-height geometry behind the report's
rank-three lattice exclusion and clarifies the rank convention. Its finite conclusion is
non-strict abundance; by itself that leaves one parity case when a deleted bottom member is
restored. Tian's strict finite theorem or the report's independent incidence proof supplies that
last step.
