## Synopsis

A noncommutative rational series is Pólya when all of its nonzero coefficients lie in one
finitely generated subgroup of the coefficient field's multiplicative group. Theorem 1.2 proves,
over a completely integrally closed domain, that this condition is equivalent to recognition by
an unambiguous weighted automaton, unambiguous rationality, an explicit exponential form with
linearly bounded rational exponents, and Hadamard subinvertibility. This resolves Reutenauer's
conjecture. The hard implication uses finiteness of nondegenerate unit equations and a linear-hull
decomposition of a minimal representation.

Theorem 1.3 characterizes deterministic weighted-automaton series over a field by a minimal
linear representation whose linear hull has dimension at most one. Corollary 1.4 makes every
finite-valued rational series deterministic. The paper treats arbitrary finite word alphabets and
both characteristic-zero and positive-characteristic coefficient fields, with the domain
hypotheses stated explicitly.

The Pólya property constrains the values of all nonzero coefficients. It does not follow merely
from a finite set of primes appearing in denominators, nor from a few coefficient subsequences.

## Source Assessment
