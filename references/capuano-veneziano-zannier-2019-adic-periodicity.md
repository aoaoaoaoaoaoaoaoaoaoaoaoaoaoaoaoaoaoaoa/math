## Synopsis

The paper gives effective finiteness and periodicity tests for Ruban ℓ-adic continued fractions
of rational and quadratic algebraic inputs. For a rational input, the expansion terminates
exactly when every complete quotient is nonnegative. Otherwise it is eventually periodic with
the constant partial quotient `ℓ − ℓ⁻¹`; the decision procedure and preperiod are effective.
The proof combines a real-sign obstruction with an explicit height bound, rather than treating
ℓ-adic convergence alone as a termination mechanism.

For a quadratic irrational, periodicity is equivalent to the existence of a unique real
embedding under which every complete quotient is positive. An explicit search bound decides
whether a sign obstruction appears or a complete quotient repeats. The paper also proves that a
periodic Ruban expansion can represent only a rational or a real quadratic number, and studies
how finiteness or periodicity varies with the prime.

The results are algorithm-specific. They do not establish a Lagrange theorem for arbitrary
ℓ-adic continued fractions, and positivity of complete quotients is essential to the effective
classification.
