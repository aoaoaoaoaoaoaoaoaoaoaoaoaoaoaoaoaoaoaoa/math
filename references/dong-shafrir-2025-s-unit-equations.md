## Results used

Theorem 1.4 makes solvability of `S`-unit equations in finitely presented
modules over

```text
ℤ/T[X₁±,…,Xₙ±]
```

Turing-equivalent to systems of linear-exponential Diophantine equations whose
exponential bases are the prime divisors of `T`. Corollary 1.5 obtains
decidability when `T` has at most two distinct prime divisors by importing the
corresponding two-prime linear-exponential theorem. The paper records the
three-or-more-prime case as open.

For the dimension-two campaign this is a scope warning: replacing the affine
word equation by an unrestricted module `S`-unit equation can discard its
monotone exponent-path constraint and land in an open general problem.

## Audit notes

The local file is arXiv v2, licensed CC BY 4.0. It is a preprint and has not
been peer reviewed.

No reduction from the mixed-prime affine benchmark to the paper's module
problem has been established here. The presence of the primes `2,3,5` does not
show that the benchmark inherits the general three-prime difficulty. This
source forbids a black-box appeal to an unavailable arbitrary-prime theorem;
it is not hardness evidence for `M₂(3)`.
