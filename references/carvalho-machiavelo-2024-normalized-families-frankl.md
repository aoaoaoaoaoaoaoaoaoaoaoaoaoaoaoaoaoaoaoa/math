## Results used

A normalized family is a separating union-closed family containing the empty set with exactly
one more member than ground-set elements. Every nonempty member has a unique root element.
The dual of a normalized family is normalized; moreover `N=J(N)**`, while `J(N)*` is
independent. Removing any minimal nonempty member and deleting the universal root from the
remaining members produces a normalized family on one fewer element. The corresponding child
operation on the independent dual reduces cardinality by one and preserves independence.

## Audit notes

The deletion recursion is structurally adjacent to Bouchard’s irreducible-deletion analysis and
the tight-family parity route. The source does not prove that abundance inequalities survive the
recursion, nor does it close the conjecture for arbitrary normalized families. It supplies the
canonical recursive setting; any frequency martingale, parity invariant, or minimal-counterexample
conclusion used here must be proved separately. No redistributable full text was located.
