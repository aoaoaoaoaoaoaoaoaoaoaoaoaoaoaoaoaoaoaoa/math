Singular Return Compression

Matrix mortality for two 9 × 9 integer matrices is undecidable: M9(2).

A primitive-recursive compiler maps each program to an integer pair whose nonempty zero products coincide with halting, using an injective tilted separator and a nine-state return realization.

This lowers the two-generator mortality bound from dimension ten to nine; zero-block padding covers every dimension at least nine, while M8(2) remains unresolved.

Known Stuff#

Paired scalar source#

The fixed-boundary construction compiles a restricted binary tag system into a scalar series over three paired controls: a phase toggle T and data roles Db, Dc. For every regular source width β and body b, a nonempty control word has zero coefficient exactly when the tag system halts.

TagHaltsFrom(β,b) ⇔ ∃w≠ε: cβ,b (w)=0.

The three controls act on a four-dimensional paired phase space. The usual scalar-to-mortality construction adjoins a rank-one outer product ux. A product containing that separator is zero exactly when the intervening paired coefficient vanishes.

Return interface#

Let A be a square transition, U an input map, and O an output map. The physical alphabet has only the transition and the cut C = UO. Runs of transitions between cuts expose the return matrices

Mn =OAnU, n∈ℕ.

Return realizations let one binary matrix pair present a larger interface alphabet. The difficulty is the converse: arbitrary products may begin or end inside a transition run, and a pure power of A must not vanish.

Previous two-generator bound#

The complete prefix compiler gave two 10 × 10 integer matrices. Exact minimization of that pair cannot reach dimension nine: its word products span the full 10 × 10 matrix algebra, and every nonzero internal-word sandwich has Hankel rank ten. The construction below changes the scalar zero series instead of compressing the ten-state pair.

New Stuff#

Result#

Rank-nine mortality theorem. There is a primitive-recursive map from program codes to pairs of 9 × 9 integer matrices such that the emitted pair is mortal exactly when the program halts. Consequently M9(2) is undecidable.

CodeHalts ≤0 Mortality(9,2), ¬ComputablePred(Mortality(9,2)).

Tilted same-zero separator#

Injective tilted code#

For the lower c-role word, let V be its base-three value and K the next power of three. The new separator row uses

q= K−3V K−3 , Fq(w) =ternaryCode(w) +q3|w|.

The restricted source syntax gives q < −3/2. At this slope, words of different lengths occupy disjoint value bands; equal-length equality reduces to ordinary base-three injectivity. Lean proves Fq injective for all finite binary words.

Both paired phases#

The row (1,q,q,q) evaluates either paired phase as the difference of two tilted codes:

(1,q,q,q) vphase (u,v) = Fq(u) − Fq(v).

Its value is zero exactly when the upper and lower words agree. Replacing the ordinary boundary row by this uniform tilted row therefore preserves every paired scalar zero, including the absorbed trailing-toggle convention. With the existing boundary column u, it defines the rank-one separator

Π~ = u(1,q,q,q).

Nine-state return chart#

Moment sequence#

An explicit rational 3+3+2+1 chain supplies a 9 × 9 transition A, a 9 × 4 input U, and a 4 × 9 output O. Its returns are

OAnU = { T,n=0, Db,n=1, Dc,n=2, sn−3R,n≥3,

Here s is nonzero and R is a nonzero scalar multiple of the tilted separator. The first two length-three chains and one length-two chain realize the exceptional moments; the final eigenline carries the geometric tail. On the audited rational chart, the transition has the equivalent block profile

A∼ J3(0) ⊕J3(0) ⊕J2(0) ⊕[s].

The chart denominators and pivots are nonzero for every emitted source. The only source-specific condition beyond the standard Neary regularity hypotheses is that the body contains b; the universal compiler proves the stronger leading-b invariant.

Pure transition words#

Let e be the basis vector of the geometric tail. The transition acts on it by the nonzero eigenvalue s, so every power remains nonzero:

Ae=se, Ane =sne≠0.

Singular return compression#

The physical generators are A and the singular cut C = UO. A general singular-return theorem absorbs the exterior transition runs and gives an exact equivalence, not only the forward encoding:

IsMortal{A,UO} ⇔ IsMortal {OAnU:n∈ℕ}.

Every return is a nonzero scaling of one of T, Db, Dc, and the tilted separator. Return times 0, 1, 2, and 3 form a right inverse to this finite relabelling. Independent nonzero scalings preserve zero products, so the return family is mortal exactly when the paired scalar series has a nonempty zero.

IsMortal{A,UO} ⇔ ∃w≠ε: cβ,b (w)=0.

Effective integer emitter#

Unreduced denominator clearing#

The rational formulas are evaluated as unreduced numerator-denominator pairs. Addition, multiplication, division, negation, and fixed powers remain primitive recursive without normalization or greatest-common-divisor computation. For each generator, the 81 entries are flattened and cleared by one recursively accumulated product of their nonzero denominators.

cast(G^i) =diGi, di≠0, G^i∈M9(ℤ).

The two generators may use different clearing factors. A product of emitted matrices is the corresponding rational product times a product of nonzero scalars, so mortality is preserved and reflected. Lean proves every fixed emitted entry primitive recursive in the source body.

Universal reduction#

The fixed universal two-tag system is compiled through Cook’s cyclic-tag construction and Neary’s restricted binary-tag construction. The source emitter is primitive recursive, its body begins with b, and its tag instance halts exactly for accepted program codes. Composition with the integer matrix emitter gives the many-one reduction in the theorem.

CodeHalts(e) ⇔ TagHaltsFrom(β,be) ⇔ IsMortal(G^e).

Consequences#

For every n ≥ 0, zero-block padding sends each 9 × 9 generator to

Gi ↦ Gi⊕0n.

This preserves every zero product and proves M9+n(2) undecidable. The two-generator frontier therefore moves from dimension ten to nine; M8(2) is the next open cell.
