# M₃(2) exact-order projective automata

Date: 2026-07-30

## Transfer

For decoded guard parameters `(p,s,A,D,L)`, the homogeneous residual transfer at wait `a` is

```text
⎡ A−Lpᵃ          D ⎤
⎣ pˢᵃ(A−L)   pˢᵃD ⎦.
```

If `ℓ` is a primitive prime divisor of `pᵉ−1`, then `p` has exact order `e` modulo `ℓ`.
Both powers in the transfer therefore depend only on `a mod e`. This gives a finite labelled
system on `ℙ¹(𝔽ℓ)` after adjoining one state for a zero homogeneous image.

## Exact cancellation

Suppose an integral step has unscaled output

```text
(m̃,ñ)=g(m′,n′),
```

where both source and reduced target pairs are primitive. Modulo `ℓ`, the transfer image is

```text
pˢᵃg · (m′,n′).
```

Since a primitive divisor never divides `p`, projectivization follows `(m′,n′)` exactly when
`ℓ∤g`. It reaches the added annihilation state exactly when `ℓ∣g`. Thus quotient cancellation
is neither a parser failure nor a loose abstraction: it is precisely cyclotomic swallowing by
primitive reduction.

## Finite invariant certificate

Let `S⊆ℙ¹(𝔽ℓ)⊔{cancelled}`. If

1. the reset ray belongs to `S`;
2. every residue transition preserves `S`;
3. `cancelled∉S`; and
4. the terminal ray does not belong to `S`,

then no primitively reduced integral execution connects reset to terminal. The Lean theorem
quantifies over all path lengths and all wait sequences.

## Period-three certificate

For

```text
p=3, s=2, A=−953, D=473, L=2240,
```

eleven is a primitive divisor of `3⁵−1`. The five residue transfers modulo eleven have first
columns

| `a mod 5` | top | bottom | image |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 8 | 8 | 1 |
| 1 | 5 | 6 | 10 |
| 2 | 7 | 10 | 4 |
| 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 9 | 7 | 6 |

Their second columns vanish because `11∣D`. Every nonzero affine ray therefore lands in

```text
{1,4,6,10}.
```

All five columns are nonzero, so cancellation is absent. The reset pair `(1,1)` represents
ray one. The terminal pair `(−473,−3193)` represents ray zero and is excluded. This is a
four-state certificate against every primitive integral terminal execution, not a verification
of only the displayed three-cycle.

The same bundle independently proves physical immortality: decoded steps are functional, the
three rational cycle states form a closed invariant, and the terminal residual lies outside it.
Thus the finite quotient is not being used to smuggle an unproved integral-to-rational converse
into the concrete no-instance.

## Reconnaissance and inference

Exact computation found other separating quotients, including `ℓ=31`, but many primitive
quotients were saturated and reached both terminal and cancellation. Finite quotient dynamics
is therefore genuinely discriminating but not automatically useful.

The modulo-eleven certificate exposes a special mechanism: a primitive quotient which also
divides the fixed drift numerator collapses every transfer to rank one. Such parameter-divisor
quotients may form a tractable certificate family distinct from generic projective dynamics.
This mechanism is now a theorem. If `A−L` and every `A−Lpʳ` over the exact period survive
modulo the divisor, the nonzero affine shell is invariant and excludes both cancellation and
the zero terminal ray. Equivalently, `A/L` lies outside the subgroup generated by `p`.

The experiment does not prove that the parameter-divisor or generic projective family is
complete. A fixed nonzero `D` has only finitely many prime divisors, and generic quotients may
be saturated.

The remaining semantic seam is also precise. The certificate theorem ranges over
`PrimitiveIntegralStep`. The project already proves one integral step realizes the rational
residual map, but has not yet packaged the converse normalization theorem saying every decoded
rational step admits a primitive integral representative. Until that lift is formalized, the
generic finite invariant should not by itself be described as a physical immortality theorem.
