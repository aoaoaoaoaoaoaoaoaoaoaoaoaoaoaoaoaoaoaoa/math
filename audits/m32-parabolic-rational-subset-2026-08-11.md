## Effective Parabolic Reduction

Let `G,H∈GL₂(ℚ)`, with nonzero row `r` and column `c`. Choose a nonzero column `d`
spanning `ker r`, extend `c,d` to rational bases `C=(c,c♯)` and `D=(d,d♯)`, and let `S`
contain every prime needed to put the four matrices and their inverses in
`GL₂(ℤ[S⁻¹])`. Then, for every positive-monoid element `p`,

```text
rpc=0
⇔ pc∈ℚ·d
⇔ D⁻¹pC preserves ℚ·e₁
⇔ D⁻¹pC∈Bₛ,                                    (1)
```

where `Bₛ` is the upper-triangular subgroup of `GL₂(ℤ[S⁻¹])`. Thus

```text
∃p∈{G,H}*, rpc=0
⇔ {G,H}* ∩ D Bₛ C⁻¹ ≠ ∅
⇔ I ∈ {G,H}*^⁻¹ D Bₛ C⁻¹.                         (2)
```

The last set is an effectively presented rational subset. `Bₛ` is finitely generated by
`U(1)`, the two diagonal signs, and diagonal multiplication by every prime in `S`, together
with inverses. Conjugating `U(1)` by those diagonal generators produces every translation in
`ℤ[S⁻¹]`.

This avoids the generally harder task of computing the stabilizer inside `⟨G,H⟩`; the
stabilizer is replaced by an explicit ambient parabolic. No generic-position, freeness,
discreteness, or affine-chart assumption enters (1).

Consequently the following external algorithmic statement would decide arbitrary PI₂, hence
normalized GPI₂:

```text
SARSM₂: for every finite S, identity membership is decidable
         for every automaton-labelled rational subset of GL₂(ℤ[S⁻¹]).
```

No such theorem is claimed. The literature comparisons in the report are retained only as
reported search evidence; the repository does not promote flat-subset, integral, or module
results beyond their stated hypotheses.

## Fixed Rank-Two Affine Cusp

For the checked shortcut-Collatz predecessor maps

```text
A(z)=2z,       B(z)=(2z−1)/3,
```

the generated projective group is exactly

```text
Γ₆ = {z↦2ᵐ3ⁿz+q : m,n∈ℤ, q∈ℤ[1/6]}
   ≅ ℤ[1/6] ⋊ ℤ².                                  (3)
```

Indeed `E=AB⁻¹` is `3z+1`; comparing `E` with its `A`-conjugate produces translation
`T(z)=z+1`, then the dilation `3z`. Conversely
`B=D₃⁻¹T⁻¹D₂`. Conjugating integral translations by arbitrary powers of the two
dilations produces all of `ℤ[1/6]`.

Let `K=Stab_Γ₆(1)≅ℤ²`, let `P={A,B}*`, and put `R=PK`. For the translation
`τₙ(z)=z+N−1`,

```text
τₙ∈R
⇔ ∃p∈P, p(1)=N
⇔ N reaches 1 under shortcut Collatz.                    (4)
```

The last equivalence is the existing kernel-checked all-word predecessor theorem: the first
illegal `B` creates permanent negative 3-adic valuation. Hence pointwise shortcut Collatz is
variable-element membership in one fixed rational subset of one fixed finitely generated
metabelian group. Central normalization then places every nonzero target in the checked
`α=β=1` GPI₂ chart.

This is stronger than saying that GPI₂ contains a Collatz-shaped family. Every general
rational-subset algorithm must terminate on the single fixed pair `(Γ₆,R)` with only `τₙ`
varying.

## What Does Not Cause The Difficulty

The positive monoid `P` is free. Every word has a unique form

```text
w(z)=(2^ℓ(w)z−mₓ)/3^k(w),       mₓ≥0.
```

Appending `A` sends `m↦2m`; appending `B` sends `m↦2m+3ᵏ`. Equality of affine maps first
recovers length and `B`-count from the multiplier, then the parity of `m` recovers the last
letter, and recursion recovers the word. Order nevertheless matters: words with the same
Parikh vector can send `1` to different points.

Thus relations, a missing positive-word normal form, and commutative exponent data are not the
obstruction. The hard operation is intersection of a free positive monoid with a parabolic
coset.

## Why The Tits Split Fails

The genuinely virtually abelian cases, such as torus normalizers, are not the issue. The affine
one-fixed-point branch includes groups whose multiplier image has rank two. Every finite-index
subgroup of `Γ₆` retains rank-two multiplier image, so it cannot become a one-dilation
Baumslag-Solitar group `ℤ[1/q]⋊ℤ` after finite index. Any theorem confined to a single
dilation misses the fixed Collatz cusp.

Nor may the affine cusp be dismissed inside a larger non-elementary group. Adjoining an
inversion creates a non-elementary modular subgroup, but a rational-subset automaton can remain
entirely inside `Γ₆`. Horospherical translation is exponentially distorted:

```text
D_q^m T D_q^⁻ᵐ = T^(q^m),       q∈{2,3}.
```

Short words therefore carry exponentially large cusp coordinates. Hyperbolic displacement
away from the cusp gives no terminal bound for the automaton inside it.

Fixed-arity `S`-unit equations also miss the problem. An affine word has an ordered translation
sum with one term per letter; both arity and constrained exponent path are unbounded. The
concrete Collatz monoid also lies outside the reported flat rational-subset class: its standard
matrix representatives have determinant magnitudes `2^|w|3^#B(w)`, whereas each flat component
over `GL₂(ℤ)` has one fixed determinant magnitude. This non-applicability argument does not
assert anything about arbitrary rational-subset membership.

## Master Implication

SARSM₂ would decide PI₂ by (2), then the checked rank-two punctuation equivalence would decide
the entire rank-(2,2) profile of `M₃(2)`. It would not decide the independently surviving
rank-(3,2) return-recurrence profile.

Conversely, undecidability of variable translation membership `τₙ∈R` would transfer through
the existing normalized incidence and reverse-edge compiler to rank-(2,2) mortality and hence
to `M₃(2)`. No such result is known or claimed; (4) is exactly pointwise shortcut Collatz.
