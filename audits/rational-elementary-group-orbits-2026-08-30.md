## Verdict

Point-to-point orbit reachability is decidable for every finitely generated subgroup of the
rational affine group, without a uniqueness promise. The same holds for every rational
projective group preserving an unordered pair of algebraic points. These two algorithms decide
all elementary branches of rational group-`PI₂`.

This is a group theorem. It does not decide positive affine-semigroup reachability. For the
mixed-prime generators `D(z)=2z/3` and `T(z)=3z/5+1`, group completion contains every translation
by `ℤ[1/30]` and erases the benchmark obstruction completely.

## Affine Translation Kernel

Write the input generators as

```text
fᵢ(z)=aᵢz+bᵢ,                 aᵢ∈ℚ×, bᵢ∈ℚ,
Γ=⟨f₁,…,fₖ⟩.
```

Let `S` contain the primes dividing the reduced numerator or denominator of some `aᵢ`, and put

```text
R=ℤ[a₁±¹,…,aₖ±¹].
```

If `a=εm/n` in lowest terms, Bézout coefficients `um+vn=1` give

```text
1/n=uεa+v,                    1/m=vεa⁻¹+u.
```

Thus `R=ℤ[S⁻¹]`; the reverse inclusion is immediate.

Let `η:ℤᵏ→ℚ×` send `e` to `∏aᵢ^eᵢ`. Its kernel has a computable integral basis: prime valuations
give integer linear equations and the rational sign gives one congruence modulo two. The kernel
of the free-group multiplier map is normally generated by

```text
[xᵢ,xⱼ]                       for all i,j,
x₁^e₁⋯xₖ^eₖ                  for a basis e of ker η.
```

Evaluate this finite list in the affine generators. Every result is a translation `τ_t`. Since

```text
f τ_t f⁻¹=τ_(a_f t),
```

normal closure proves the exact identity

```text
N={t∈ℚ:τ_t∈Γ}=Σ Rt,
```

where the sum ranges over those finitely many relator translations. Both containments matter:
the relators generate the complete multiplier kernel, and every `R`-linear combination is a
finite product of conjugates and integral powers of their translations.

Because `R` is a localization of `ℤ`, every finitely generated rational `R`-submodule is one
principal fractional ideal. A common denominator followed by an integer gcd computes

```text
N=nR
```

or proves `N=0`.

## Nontrivial-Kernel Algorithm

Let

```text
T=R-span{p,q,b₁,…,bₖ}=tR.
```

Every relator translation lies in the `R`-span of the offsets, so `N⊆T`. Every generator and its
inverse preserve both `T` and `N`. If `N≠0`, then

```text
T/N ≅ R/(n/t)R.
```

After multiplying `n/t` by an `S`-unit, remove its `S`-prime factors to obtain an integer
`d>0`, coprime to every prime in `S`, with `(n/t)R=dR`. Localization then gives

```text
T/N ≅ ℤ/dℤ.
```

Breadth-first search in this explicit finite quotient decides whether `q+N` belongs to the orbit
of `p+N`. The quotient test is exact. If `g(p)-q=δ∈N`, then `τ_(-δ)∈Γ` by the definition of `N`,
and `τ_(-δ)g` sends `p` to `q`. No multiplier-coset condition remains hidden after the finite
search.

## Trivial-Kernel Algorithm

If `N=0`, the multiplier projection `Γ→ℚ×` is injective, so `Γ` is abelian. A generator with
multiplier one must be the identity. If all generators are identities, reachability is equality.
Otherwise choose `fⱼ` with `aⱼ≠1` and set

```text
r=bⱼ/(1-aⱼ).
```

Commutativity gives

```text
(1-aᵢ)bⱼ=(1-aⱼ)bᵢ,
```

so every generator fixes `r` and every `g∈Γ` acts as

```text
g(z)=r+μ(g)(z-r).
```

The orbit of `r` is a singleton. For `p,q≠r`, reachability is equivalent to

```text
(q-r)/(p-r) ∈ ⟨a₁,…,aₖ⟩ ≤ ℚ×.
```

Prime valuations and the sign congruence reduce this membership test to one Smith-normal-form
integer system.

## Projective Elementary Groups

A rational projective group with a common rational fixed point is conjugate to the affine group.
The fixed point is a singleton orbit; all other endpoints enter the affine algorithm above.

If the group preserves an unordered pair `{α,β}`, use

```text
θ(z)=(z-α)/(z-β).
```

Every pair-preserving projectivity acts by `θ↦χθ` or `θ↦χ/θ`. Split the index-at-most-two swap
parity. Point reachability becomes membership of `θ(q)/θ(p)` or `θ(q)θ(p)` in the finitely
generated multiplicative subgroup generated by the `χ` values. For a split rational pair this
is the rational sign-and-valuation test. For a nonsplit conjugate quadratic pair, principal-ideal
valuations reduce the test to the effectively computable quadratic unit group, whose free rank is
at most one. Rational endpoints cannot equal either nonsplit point. A supplied invariant pair can
therefore be decided directly; detection reduces to exact degree-two invariant-set equations,
with cyclic involutions and finite groups handled directly.

## Mixed-Prime Benchmark

For affine multiplication as composition,

```text
[(a,b),(c,d)] = τ_((a-1)d+(1-c)b).
```

Hence

```text
[D,T]=τ_(-1/3).
```

The multiplier relation lattice of `2/3` and `3/5` is zero, while `-1/3` is a unit in
`R=ℤ[1/30]`. Therefore `N=R`: every two points of `R` lie in the same group orbit. Any successful
analysis of the mixed-prime mortality shell must retain positivity.

## Exact Non-Elementary Survivor

After enlarging `S` and conjugating the source point to infinity, the unresolved group-`PI₂`
branch is exactly

```text
UCB₂(S): decide ⟨A,B⟩ ∩ g₀B_S ≠ ∅,
           promised |⟨A,B⟩ ∩ g₀B_S|≤1,
```

where

```text
B_S=Stab(∞)∩PGL₂(ℤ[S⁻¹]) ≅ ℤ[S⁻¹]⋊ℤ[S⁻¹]×.
```

The group `B_S` is effectively finitely generated by translation by one, sign, and dilation by
the primes in `S`. Equivalently, `UCB₂(S)` asks whether the identity belongs to

```text
(A|A⁻¹|B|B⁻¹)* g₀ B_S.
```

Flat rational-subset algorithms do not absorb the first star. In a faithful free branch, every
finite union of bounded products of cyclic stars has polynomial word growth, while the free
group has exponential growth. Finite congruence or Bruhat–Tits truncations are also incomplete:
`R32-O22` is a promised empty intersection with a projective hit at every modulus, and its
positive modular lift gives simultaneous positive representatives at every finite family of
depths. The uniqueness promise says only that a successful target stabilizer is trivial; it gives
no regular normal form or length bound.

The positive binary transfer is a different seam. `M4-D04` decides every fixed-boundary
free-group instance for which either binary morphism is noninjective, so its hard branch is
injective/injective. On that branch, the formalized exponent obstruction shows that the third
triangle control `(yx)⁻¹` cannot be replaced by a positive word in the first two controls through
a faithful representation. Any binary transfer must therefore exploit cancellation or boundary
structure; neither group completion nor literal positive generator elimination supplies it.
