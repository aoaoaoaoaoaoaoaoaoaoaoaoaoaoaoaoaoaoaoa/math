## Completion

Write each rule as `ℓ→r`. Every rule has `|ℓ|=|r|` and `ℓ>r` in lexicographic order. A reduction
therefore remains in one finite set `{D,T}ⁿ` and strictly descends, so no infinite reduction exists.
The `45` adjoined rules are oriented branch pairs of critical overlaps among the five base rules;
each already belongs to the base congruence, so adjoining them does not change that congruence.

The completed left sides are pairwise distinct. No left side occurs properly in another left side,
so there are no inclusion ambiguities. Exact enumeration finds `450` proper suffix-prefix
overlaps among the `50` left sides, with source lengths from `52` through `88`. All join:

- `45` are the first-generation pairs adjoined as rules and have trace lengths `(0,1)` up to
  orientation;
- `405` are new overlaps and have trace lengths `(0,2)` up to orientation.

Disjoint redexes commute. Inclusion and overlap are the only remaining local branchings for a
string-rewrite system, so the enumerated joins prove local confluence. Termination and Newman's
lemma give confluence. Hence every conversion class has one irreducible normal form.

The certificate also checks every rule by an exact integer affine signature. For a word `w`, the
signature `(A,B,Q)` represents the upper-triangular matrix

```text
[[A,B],[0,Q]].
```

The recurrence uses arbitrary-precision integers and checks `(A,B,Q)(ℓ)=(A,B,Q)(r)` for every
rule and both branches of every critical peak. Thus every completed rewrite remains inside the
mixed-prime affine kernel.

the two odd-family words have the form


No completed left side occurs in either word. This is a finite statement despite unbounded `k`.
An occurrence wholly inside `(DT)ᵏ` cannot begin `TTDD`. Every other factor of length at most `59`

once `k≥59`; the certificate checks all depths `1≤k≤59`, which therefore cover every boundary
factor at all larger depths. It finds no redex. Since `Uₖ` begins in `D`, `Vₖ` begins in `T`, and
both are irreducible, unique normal forms prove


in the five-rule congruence for every `k≥1`. The affine kernel is strictly larger than the finite
completed quotient.
