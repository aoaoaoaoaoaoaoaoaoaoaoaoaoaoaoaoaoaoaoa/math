Verdict

A matrix family consisting of arbitrary transitions and factored cuts is mortal exactly when the transition family is already mortal or one nonempty path of compressed interface bridges has zero product.

The theorem requires no field, splitting, rank calculation, or invertibility assumption. Rank-one separators, singular-return compression, split-return normal forms, and multi-cut edge systems are instances of the same reduction.

Known Stuff#

Factored cuts#

Let L be the physical state space and S a smaller interface space. The generator family contains arbitrary transition matrices Aa on L and cuts

Cj = Uj Oj, Uj:S→L, Oj:L→S.

No minimal factorization is required. In particular, S need not have dimension equal to the rank of the cut.

Physical words#

A word with cuts alternates transition blocks and cuts, but it may also contain transitions before its first cut and after its last. A correct mortality reduction must retain both exterior blocks and must separately handle words containing no cut.

For a transition word w, define the bridge from cut i to cut j by

Bi,w,j = Oi Aw Uj :S→S.

New Stuff#

General theorem#

Interface compression. Over any commutative semiring, the family of transitions and factored cuts is mortal if and only if either the transitions alone are mortal or there are cuts j0, …, jr and transition words w1, …, wr, with r ≥ 1, such that

∏t=1r Ojt−1 Awt Ujt =0.

Proof#

A cut-free zero word is already a zero transition word. Suppose instead that a physical zero contains a first and a last cut. Decompose it into its leading transition block and cut-headed blocks. Multiplying on the left by the first cut’s output map and on the right by the last cut’s input map turns the zero physical product into a zero product of interface bridges. The leading and trailing transition blocks become the closing bridge, so neither is discarded.

Conversely, spell a bridge path as a physical word beginning with its first cut. Associativity gives

physical product = Uj0 (bridge-path product) Ojr.

A zero bridge path therefore gives an actual nonempty zero word in the original generators.

Rank-one interfaces#

When S is one-dimensional, write each cut as cjrjT. Every bridge is the scalar

riT Aw cj.

Over a field, a product of one-dimensional bridges vanishes exactly when one bridge vanishes. Thus mortality reduces to one row–word–column incidence. The repeated rank-one separator is the one-cut instance.

Single-cut return families#

With one cut C = UO, the bridge alphabet is the complete return family

{OAwU:w∈Σ*}.

If there is one transition A, these are the moments Mn = OAnU. Hence

Mortal{A,UO} ⇔ (∃n≥1:An=0) ∨ Mortal{Mn:n≥0}.

The M₉(2) construction excludes the pure-transition branch with a nonzero eigenline, leaving the exact singular-return equivalence.

Many cuts#

Several cuts form a directed graph. Vertices are cut interfaces; a bridge from i to j is labelled by a transition word and carries the matrix OiAwUj. Mortality is exactly zero-product reachability along a nonempty path, together with the separate transition-only branch.

Verification and provenance#

General compression

InterfaceCompression.isMortal_iff proves the transition-or-path equivalence over an arbitrary commutative semiring.

Rank-one specialization

InterfaceCompression.isMortal_rankOne_iff reduces any finite collection of rank-one cuts to one scalar bridge incidence.

Transitive axioms

Both declarations use only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound.

Formal scope#

The physical spelling, arbitrary-word block decomposition, exterior-block sandwich, bridge-path converse, general theorem, and rank-one scalar specialization are machine-checked. The theorem assumes neither minimal cut rank nor nonvanishing transition powers; those enter only in applications that remove one branch of the disjunction.
